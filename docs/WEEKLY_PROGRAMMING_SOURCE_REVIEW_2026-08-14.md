## 1. What this source owns

This source owns:

- required strength-session counts and purposes;
- strength, conditioning, sprint, power, club-training and game placement;
- hard-day and rest-day spacing;
- weekly upper/lower and movement-pattern distribution;
- approximate working-set budgets;
- every base layout for phase × available LFA gym days.

It does not choose exact exercises, equipment substitutions, injury/readiness reductions, block progression from completed training, or athlete-facing wording.

Today:

```text
weekly planner → strength composer → conditioning/power policies
→ safety validator → athlete display
```

Target:

```text
ONE approved weekly contract → ONE weekly scheduler
→ composition → safety modifiers → athlete display
```

No later layer may silently redesign the week.

## 2. Definitions — resolved

### Available LFA gym days — GOOD

“Available days” are the days on which the athlete can get to a gym or their usual strength equipment. These days permit the app to schedule required LFA strength work or equipment-based erg work.

It does not mean total active days. Club training and games are separate anchors.

The app must not place required strength sessions outside these days. Equipment-free running or conditioning—required or optional—may be placed on other days when needed to satisfy the phase contract. Equipment-free mobility, recovery and accessories may also be offered outside gym-access days. None of those sessions becomes an LFA gym day.

Any work placed outside gym-access days must still respect game and club-training placement, hard-day and recovery spacing, injury/readiness constraints and any day the athlete has explicitly marked unavailable.

### Strength session versus movement exposure — GOOD

A **strength session** is one scheduled strength workout. A full-body workout still counts as one strength session, regardless of how many patterns it trains.

A **movement exposure** is one meaningful stimulus for a movement pattern inside a strength session.

For a healthy no-game pre-season athlete:

- Prefer **4 separate strength sessions** when availability permits.
- Two available gym days means two full-body strength sessions.
- Three available gym days means three strength sessions.
- Four or more available gym days normally allows four strength sessions.
- Availability is permission, not a quota. Five or six available days do not create five or six required strength sessions.

Across the complete week, aim for at least one meaningful exposure in each pattern:

- horizontal push;
- horizontal pull;
- vertical push;
- vertical pull;
- squat;
- hinge;
- single-leg knee;
- single-leg hip.

Additional exposures remain balanced with their partner:

- horizontal push ↔ horizontal pull;
- vertical push ↔ vertical pull;
- squat ↔ hinge;
- single-leg knee ↔ single-leg hip.

For example, a second vertical pull requires a second vertical push. A second squat requires a second hinge. Balance is measured across the whole week, not necessarily inside each session.

Equipment, injury or another authorised constraint may make a pattern impossible. The app records and explains that gap rather than prescribing illegal work.

## 3. Global weekly rules

| Area | Approved rule |
| --- | --- |
| Game | Main in-season anchor. Build the week for freshness on game day. |
| Club training | Use the athlete’s real nights; never assume Tuesday/Thursday. |
| Rest | Normally 1–2 full rest days. Early off-season and bye-recovery may have 3. Optional recovery still counts as rest. |
| Hard-day total | Prefer 4 hard days; allow 5. Count days, not sessions. The app does not program 6 hard days. An athlete may choose a sixth after a warning. |
| Consecutive hard days | Prefer no more than 3. Four is acceptable when availability requires it. Five is allowed only when followed by two complete rest days, such as Monday–Friday training with the weekend off. |
| Lower spacing | At least one complete day between hard lower sessions; prefer two or more. Never place lower strength sessions on consecutive days. |
| Repeated patterns | Do not repeat the same movement plane on consecutive days: horizontal push→horizontal push, vertical pull→vertical pull, squat→squat, hinge→hinge, single-leg knee→single-leg knee, and so on. Different planes in the same broad family may follow each other—for example, horizontal push followed by vertical push. The same exercise should not normally appear on consecutive days. Weekly paired-pattern balance remains a separate rule. |
| Running spacing | No more than 3 running days consecutively. |
| Compatible doubles | Strength + conditioning doubles are encouraged when they create genuine rest days. |
| Bad double | Do not stack a complete lower session and complete upper session. Use one properly sized full-body session instead. |
| Hinge + sprint | Never pair a lower hinge session with oval sprint work. |
| Upper + running | Hard running/top-end work belongs with upper days where possible. |
| Lower + conditioning | Prefer off-leg work: bike, ski, rower or assault bike. |
| Club-day gym | Gym may share a club-training day. Use regular loads; do not reduce solely because club training is later. Exercise selection may still avoid needless soreness. |
| G-2 | No heavy lower-body or added speed work. **Sam, 2026-08-15: *"should not rule out upper body power"*** — G-2 prohibits added LOWER-BODY sprint, jumping, plyometric and power work. **Upper-body power is permitted as a COMPONENT of an already authorised upper-body strength session.** It never creates a session, moves a day, or counts as conditioning. |
| G-1 | No heavy lifting or conditioning. Optional Gunshow, accessories or recovery are acceptable. |
| G+1 | Rest or recovery. |
| Lower frequency | Two lower exposures is a good year-round target when availability and phase allow it. In-season may consolidate the weekly lower patterns into one lower session or two full-body sessions. |
| Upper frequency | Aim for 2–3 upper exposures per week. |
| Conditioning | Aim for 3–5 total depending on phase. Club training and games count. Total cap 5; a fifth is off-leg. |
| Running | Minimum 2, preferred 3, maximum 4. Club training counts. |
| Sprint/high-speed | At least 1 except early off-season. Games and club training can supply it. In-season, only add sprint work when there is no club training, and place it G-3 or earlier. |
| Full body | Exact rows depend on what the whole week still needs. It is not a fixed seven-exercise template. |
| Lower balance | Match squat with hinge and single-leg knee with single-leg hip across the week. Two of one pair and one of the other pair is acceptable: e.g. squat/hinge ×2 with single-leg knee/hip ×1, or vice versa. |
| Session size | Seven app-authored strength movements is a daily ceiling, not a target. Weekly movement coverage, balanced workload and even distribution matter more. Mobility does not count. |
| Athlete authority | The generated program follows these rules. The athlete may add or remove work after receiving a clear warning about unusually low or high workload; the athlete has the final say. |

## 5. Workload and session size

### Exercise-category dose rules — GOOD

| Category | Normal dose | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main lift | 3–5 sets of 3–6 reps | Normally 9–25 total reps per exercise. 5×5 is preferred over 4×8 at the hard end for most of the year. 4×8 is an off-season-only hard cap. |
| Secondary compound / single-leg | 2–4 sets of 3–10 reps | 6–30 total reps per exercise; 30 is the hard cap. If main-lift work is high, secondary lifts may drop to 2 sets. |
| Accessory | 1–3 sets of 10–20 reps | Higher repetitions are acceptable for smaller muscle groups. Accessory volume falls when main and secondary work is high. |
| Nordic | Speciality work | Maximum 15 total reps in one session. A Nordic may replace the single-leg-knee slot as the one approved exception. |
| Kettlebell swing | Speciality work | Maximum 30 total reps in one session. Stop before speed or quality falls. |

### Total session workload — GOOD

For Lower, Upper and Full Body strength sessions:

- Prefer **12–15 working sets across main and secondary lifts**.
- **16 main/secondary working sets is a hard ceiling**.
- Warm-ups, core, mobility, prehab and isolation accessories sit outside that count and follow their own dose rules. They must not be used to disguise additional main or secondary work.
- The same main/secondary limit applies to Lower, Upper and Full Body sessions.
- The deliberately reduced 10-set Lower Squat and Lower Hinge sessions used in the four-session in-season layout are an approved exception below the preferred range, not a failure to reach it.

Example lower session:

```text
Back Squat             3 × 3
Romanian Deadlift      3 × 3
Walking Lunge          3 × 6
Single-Leg RDL         3 × 6
Copenhagen Plank       2 × 30 seconds
Calf Raise             2 × 20
Main/secondary total  12 working sets
Whole example         16 working sets
```

For an in-season week containing separate Lower Squat and Lower Hinge sessions, each lower session receives **10 working sets across main and secondary compound lifts**:

```text
Main lift              3 sets
Paired main lift       3 sets
Single-leg knee        2 sets
Single-leg hip         2 sets
Main/secondary total  10 sets
```

Accessory and core work does not count against that 10-set main/secondary budget, but it still contributes to the overall session workload.

Upper-body sessions may contain multiple exercises from the same broad family when they use meaningfully different angles or roles. Bench Press, Overhead Press, Half-Kneeling Dumbbell Press and Single-Arm Floor Press may coexist. Chin-Ups, Lat Pulldowns, Bent-Over Rows and Single-Arm Rows may also coexist.

Lower-body sessions should not double up closely related bilateral main lifts such as Back Squat and Front Squat. The remaining lower-body work should instead provide the corresponding hinge and single-leg patterns.

Gunshow examples:

- 2 biceps + 2 triceps + 2 shoulder movements × 2 sets = 12 working sets; or
- 1 biceps + 1 triceps + 1 shoulder movement × 3 sets = 9 working sets.

Balance biceps, triceps and shoulders across the week. Also balance calves, glutes, tibialis, hips, hamstrings, quadriceps and groin accessory work as evenly as possible.

## 7. Reference weeks

These are reference layouts, not fixed weekday templates. If an example weekday is unavailable, preserve the structure and place it on the best legal selected day.

### In-season — Saturday game, Tuesday/Thursday club training

#### Option 1 — three strength sessions

```yaml
Monday: Lower + optional off-leg conditioning
Tuesday: Upper Pull + Club Training
Wednesday: Rest, optional accessories/mobility, or Monday's missed Lower only if Wednesday remains legal; otherwise skip it
Thursday: Upper Push + Club Training
Friday: Optional Gunshow or recovery
Saturday: Game
Sunday: Rest or recovery
```

#### Option 2 — four strength sessions

```yaml
Monday: Lower Squat — 10 main/secondary sets + accessories/core as needed + optional off-leg conditioning
Tuesday: Upper Pull + Club Training
Wednesday: Lower Hinge — 10 main/secondary sets + accessories/core as needed + optional off-leg conditioning
Thursday: Upper Push + Club Training
Friday: Optional Gunshow or recovery
Saturday: Game
Sunday: Rest or recovery
```

#### Option 3 — two strength sessions

```yaml
Monday: Full Body + optional off-leg conditioning
Tuesday: Club Training
Wednesday: Full Body + optional off-leg conditioning
Thursday: Club Training
Friday: Optional Gunshow or recovery
Saturday: Game
Sunday: Rest or recovery
```

Selection:

- 2 available gym days → Option 3.
- 3 available gym days → Option 1.
- 4 or more gym days → Option 2 when the athlete is 27 or younger, or when they consistently complete Option 1 comfortably with high readiness and low fatigue. Otherwise use Option 1.
- Availability above four never creates another required strength session.
- Low readiness never triggers Option 2; it supports maintaining or reducing workload.

### Pre-season — two club nights with weekend available

```yaml
Monday: Upper Pull + Club Training
Tuesday: Lower
Wednesday: Club Training
Thursday: Rest
Friday: Upper + hard conditioning/top-end
Saturday: Lower + off-leg conditioning
Sunday: Rest
```

### Pre-season — weekend unavailable

```yaml
Monday: Upper Pull + Club Training
Tuesday: Lower Squat + off-leg conditioning
Wednesday: Club Training
Thursday: Upper Push
Friday: Lower Hinge + off-leg conditioning
Saturday: Unavailable/full rest
Sunday: Unavailable/full rest
```

Five consecutive active days are allowed here because they are followed by two full rest days.

### Pre-season — five gym days, no club training

```yaml
Monday: Upper Pull + hard conditioning/top-end
Tuesday: Lower Squat + off-leg conditioning
Wednesday: Rest
Thursday: Upper Push
Friday: Lower Hinge + off-leg conditioning
Saturday: Aerobic conditioning or mobility
Sunday: Rest
```

This provides four strength sessions and four conditioning exposures. The fifth available gym day does not create a fifth strength session.

### Off-season — general strength and fitness

```yaml
Monday: Lower + off-leg conditioning
Tuesday: Upper + running conditioning
Wednesday: Rest
Thursday: Lower + off-leg conditioning
Friday: Upper + running/top-end conditioning
Saturday: Long slow conditioning
Sunday: Rest
```

This is the default mid/late off-season week for an athlete with four available gym days and no overriding weakness or goal.

## 8. Phase overlays

The available gym days choose the base strength skeleton. Subphases change requiredness, dose and conditioning—not the identity of the scheduling owner.

### Off-season weeks 1–2 — recovery/optional block

- Every session is optional; zero completed sessions is valid.
- Strength uses higher-rep work and the approved 75% load adjustment.
- No running is required.
- Conditioning is light aerobic/off-leg work only.
- Mobility and recovery are encouraged.
- Up to three full rest days are allowed.

### Off-season weeks 3–4 — transition block

- The normal strength skeleton becomes required again.
- Strength uses the approved 90% load adjustment.
- Conditioning returns progressively through aerobic-base and controlled capacity work.
- Week 4 is not automatically a deload.
- Do not jump directly from the optional block into full late-off-season running volume.

### Off-season week 5 onward — normal build

- Normal strength loading returns.
- Use 2–4 required strength sessions according to gym availability; normally use 3–4 when availability permits.
- Conditioning builds toward 3–5 weekly exposures.
- Include at least one genuine sprint/high-speed exposure.
- Strength moves from 8–12-rep work toward 6–8 reps as pre-season approaches.
- Normal 3–4-week deload cycles begin.
- The athlete's stated weakness may change emphasis, but not create another scheduling system.

### Pre-season

- Prefer four strength sessions in a healthy no-game week when gym availability permits.
- Scale honestly to two or three strength sessions when that is all the athlete can do.
- Aim for four total conditioning exposures; club training counts.
- Use no more than two lower sessions.
- Earlier pre-season is longer/lighter; later pre-season is shorter, lower-volume and more intense.

### In-season

- Maintain strength and conditioning while arriving fresh for the game.
- No scheduled calendar deload.
- Game, club training, readiness and injury drive reductions.
- Use the two-, three- or four-session reference structure selected above.
- With at least four gym-access days, use four sessions for athletes aged 27 or younger, or for athletes consistently completing three sessions comfortably with high readiness and low fatigue. Otherwise three remains the default. Readiness and game-freshness modifiers may still reduce the selected base.

## 9. Final decisions

### Approved decisions

1. **Available days:** equipment-access gym days for required LFA strength/erg placement, not total active days.
2. **Strength versus movement exposure:** a session counts once; movement patterns are counted separately across the week.
3. **Pre-season strength frequency:** prefer four when available; scale to two or three without cramming.
4. **Weekly movement minimum:** cover all eight named patterns when achievable and keep paired-pattern repeats balanced.
5. **Lower spacing:** one complete day minimum, two or more preferred.
6. **Consecutive hard days:** prefer three; allow four for availability; allow five only before two full rest days.
7. **Running streak:** no more than three consecutive running days.
8. **In-season layout shapes:** two days = Full Body ×2; three days = Lower + Upper Pull + Upper Push; four days adds the second lower session.
9. **Pre-season layouts:** all 2–5-day shapes and the club/no-club/weekend variants are stated above.
10. **Off-season layouts:** 2, 3 and 4 required strength-session shapes are stated; extra availability does not add required sessions.
11. **Six available days:** no separate six-session layout; the phase target chooses the count.
12. **Off-season progression:** early optional → transition → normal build is stated above.
13. **Two-lower in-season volume:** 10 main/secondary working sets per lower session, then accessories/core within the overall workload guidance.
14. **In-season fourth-session selector:** with four or more gym-access days, use four sessions when the athlete is 27 or younger, or when they consistently complete three sessions comfortably with high readiness and low fatigue. The fourth session must still satisfy game-freshness and spacing rules. Low readiness never adds work.
15. **Required running outside gym days:** required equipment-free running or conditioning may be placed outside gym-access days when needed to meet the phase minimum, subject to all scheduling, safety and explicit-unavailability constraints.
16. **Working-set limit:** prefer 12–15 main/secondary working sets and never exceed 16 in a Lower, Upper or Full Body session. Accessories, core and preparation work retain their own dose rules and cannot disguise extra main/secondary work.
17. **Consecutive upper patterns:** the restriction applies to the same plane, not the broad push/pull family. Horizontal push may precede vertical push, and horizontal pull may precede vertical pull. The same plane—and normally the same exercise—must not repeat on consecutive days.

**Open coaching questions: none.** The next review is a consistency check of the complete document, not another round of programming decisions.
