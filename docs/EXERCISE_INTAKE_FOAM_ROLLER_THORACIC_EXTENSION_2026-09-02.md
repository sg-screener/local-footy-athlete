## 14. Foam Roller Thoracic Extension

- **Video:** https://youtube.com/shorts/9Hfy7ojEt18?si=WiIaDH0CmWP1tr-a
- **Category:** Mobility.
- **Main muscles:** Lats; upper back.
- **Secondary muscles:** Chest; triceps; shoulders; midline.
- **Primary cue:** Reach the weight overhead as your upper back extends.
- **Secondary cue:** Keep ribs tucked and avoid arching through the lower back.
- **Equipment:** Foam roller AND one light dumbbell OR one weight plate. The foam roller remains mandatory; beginners may perform the movement without the loading implement.
- **Unilateral:** No; repetitions are total.
- **Experience:** Everyone; use no weight as the beginner regression.
- **Session use:** Upper-body warm-up; thoracic mobility; overhead preparation; Mobility; Recovery.
- **Availability:** Automatic in upper-body Movement Prep, Mobility and Recovery when the required support is available; manual Add/Swap; never automatic in Primer.
- **Season:** All phases.
- **Near-game suitability:** Good; suitable at G-1 when performed lightly and within a comfortable range.
- **Prescription:** 2 × 5 slow reps; pause 5 seconds overhead; 30 seconds rest.
- **Loading:** One light dumbbell or weight plate held in both hands. Record total load. The weight assists the stretch rather than turning it into strength work.
- **Loading demand:** Low.
- **Fatigue:** Low.
- **Soreness:** Low.
- **Balance/stabilisation demand:** Low.
- **Eccentric demand:** Low.

**Specific painful movements / restrictions:** Loaded overhead shoulder
flexion; thoracic extension; direct pressure from the roller; gripping; rib
flare; compensating through the lower back. Reduce the load or range if the
stretch is felt at the front of the shoulder.
